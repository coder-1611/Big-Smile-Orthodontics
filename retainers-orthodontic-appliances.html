01 — Why retention matters

Keeping the smile you earned.

Prevents relapse · Fixed or removable · Planned early

When braces come off or the last aligner comes out, retention begins — and it matters as much as treatment did. Teeth sit in bone held by ligaments, and both need months to firm up around their new positions. Until they do, teeth drift back. That's orthodontic relapse, and a retainer prevents it.

02 — Your retainer options

Three ways to hold the line.

Hawley · Clear thermoplastic · Bonded lingual

Hawley retainers. An acrylic plate shaped to your palate with a wire across the front. Adjustable, repairable and durable — but the wire shows, and speech takes a week to settle.

Clear thermoplastic (Essix) retainers. Thin transparent shells covering the arch, like an Invisalign tray — nearly invisible, effortless at bedtime. They can't be adjusted, thin out over years, and warp permanently in heat.

Fixed, bonded retainers. A slim steel wire bonded behind the front teeth: nothing to remember, nothing to lose. Usually the lower front teeth — the most relapse-prone. Flossing needs a threader.

  • Your case — Rotations and closed gaps rebound hardest, often earning a wire.
  • Both together — A bonded lower wire plus a clear upper retainer.
03 — Growth appliances

Expanders and space maintainers.

Palatal expanders · Space maintainers · Early timing

Not every appliance comes after treatment. When an upper jaw is too narrow for its teeth, a palatal expander widens it. The jaw is two halves joined by a suture down the palate; in children that seam hasn't fused, so gentle pressure eases the halves apart and new bone fills in. Expanders don't hurt — expect pressure after a turn and a day of odd speech.

A space maintainer does something different. When a baby tooth is lost early, neighbours close the gap the adult tooth needs; this small fixed appliance holds that space until the permanent tooth arrives — prevention that can spare a child extractions or braces.

  • Turning at home — We show you how and when, here in Round Rock.
  • Sticky foods out — Caramel, gum and hard candy loosen cemented appliances.

04 — Wear, care & repairs

How long, and how to look after it.

Full-time first · Then nights · Bring it to every visit

Most patients wear a removable retainer full-time at first — out only for meals and brushing — then taper to nights once things are stable. Plan on nights indefinitely: teeth shift slowly for life, and the habit holds your result.

Rinse it in cool water whenever it comes out, brush gently with a soft toothbrush and clear liquid soap, and keep it away from heat. Store it in its case, never a napkin.

Lost it, cracked it, or felt a bonded wire come loose? Call our Round Rock office at (512) 828-7900 — teeth move within weeks. And if an old retainer no longer fits, don't force it in: your teeth have moved, and it can tip or overload them. Bring it in for a quick iTero scan. Costs are quoted at the visit.

Questions, answered.

How long do I have to wear my retainer?

Full-time for the first several months, then nights only — indefinitely. Teeth shift slowly for life, so the nightly habit protects your result.

Can I use an old retainer to realign my teeth?

No — and don't force one in. Retainers hold teeth still; they don't move them, and an old one no longer matches your mouth. Bring it in for a scan.

What happens if I stop wearing my retainer?

Teeth drift back toward their old positions — orthodontic relapse. It shows first as slight crowding in the lower front teeth; caught early, a new retainer usually settles it.

Hawley or clear retainer — which is better?

Both work. Hawley retainers are adjustable and durable, but the wire shows. Clear ones are nearly invisible but can't be adjusted and need periodic replacement.

Do fixed retainers stay in forever?

They can last many years, and some keep theirs indefinitely. We check the bond at every visit, since a wire can loosen on hard foods.

How do I clean my retainer?

Rinse in cool water each time it comes out, then brush gently with a soft toothbrush and clear liquid soap. Skip toothpaste and heat, which scratch and warp them.
