How to read these

What you're actually looking at.

Every case on this page is a real patient of Dr. Saba Asrar, treated at our Round Rock office. The photographs are taken at the same angles at the start and end of treatment, under the same lighting, with no retouching of the teeth themselves. The timeline listed on each card is that patient's actual treatment length, not an average.

Before-and-after photos are the most honest thing an orthodontist can show you, but they need context to be useful. A twelve-month Invisalign case and a twenty-two-month braces case can look equally dramatic in a photo while representing completely different clinical problems. Here is what the labels mean:

The treatment types

Invisalign cases were treated with clear aligners, which suit mild to moderate crowding, spacing, and many bite issues — and are the most popular choice among our adult patients. Metal braces handle the most complex movements: severe rotation, significant bite correction, and cases needing precise control over individual teeth. Ceramic braces do the same clinical work as metal with tooth-coloured brackets, so they show up in cases where a patient wanted the strength of braces without the visibility.

The clinical problems

Crowding is the most common reason people come to see us: not enough room in the arch, so teeth overlap and rotate. Gaps are the opposite problem, and often the fastest to close — several cases here finished in under a year. Bite correction is the category that matters most for long-term dental health, covering overbite, underbite, crossbite, and open bite. A bad bite wears enamel unevenly, strains the jaw joint, and makes teeth harder to keep clean, which is why we treat it even when a patient's front teeth already look straight.

Why your timeline will be different

Treatment length depends on how far your teeth need to travel, whether the jaw relationship needs correcting as well as the teeth, your age, and — more than anything else — how consistently you wear what we give you. Aligner patients who hit 20 to 22 hours a day finish close to the estimate; patients who do not, do not. Adults sometimes take slightly longer than teenagers because bone remodels more slowly after growth has finished, though the end result is the same.

What we will not do is quote you someone else's timeline. At your free consultation Dr. Asrar examines your teeth, takes X-rays and a 3D digital scan, and gives you a plan built on your anatomy — with a written price and a realistic finish date before you commit to anything.

Keeping the result

None of these outcomes stay put on their own. Teeth have a memory, and without retention they drift back toward where they started — a process called relapse. Every patient here finished treatment with a retainer and instructions for wearing it, which is genuinely the difference between a result that lasts three years and one that lasts thirty.
