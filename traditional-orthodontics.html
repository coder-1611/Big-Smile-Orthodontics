01 — What modern metal braces are

The classic, rebuilt.

Smaller brackets · Gentler wires · Every kind of case

If the braces you picture are the bulky bands your parents wore, today's are unrecognizable. Brackets are a fraction of the old size and sit flat against the tooth, and archwires are heat-activated nickel-titanium — a metal that flexes at body temperature and applies light, continuous pressure instead of the hard tightening older braces needed.

Metal braces are still the most versatile tool in orthodontics. Because each bracket is bonded on, treatment runs around the clock whether or not you remember anything, and we can move a tooth several ways at once — rotating a stubborn canine or uprighting a tilted molar.

02 — How treatment actually goes

Bracket, wire, patience.

Free consult · Bonding day · Quick adjustments

Everything begins with a free consultation at our Round Rock office. Dr. Asrar examines your teeth and bite, reviews X-rays and a scan, and shows you what needs to move and why. You leave with a plan, a timeline, and an exact price.

Bonding day takes about an hour, with no drilling and no shots. Brackets are placed with a tooth-colored adhesive and set with a curing light, then one archwire is threaded across them and held with elastic ties — the part where you pick colors.

After that you'll come in every six to eight weeks for a short adjustment — a fresh wire, new ties, or elastics to change how your upper and lower teeth meet. Expect two or three days of tenderness after bonding and after big wire changes. If teeth feel slightly loose, that's normal — here's why.

Some cases use self-ligating brackets, which hold the wire with a sliding gate rather than ties — less to trap food, and often shorter visits. When your bite is finished, the braces come off in one appointment and we scan for retainers the same day. More on daily life in for patients.

03 — Who braces suit best

When metal is the better answer.

Complex bites · Kids & teens · Adults of any age

Braces suit growing kids and teens, whose tooth movement can be coordinated with jaw growth, and plenty of adults who want the most predictable route to a finished bite.

The clearest case for braces is a complicated one. Clear aligners are excellent at what they do, but severely rotated teeth, large vertical movements, deep bites, and pronounced open bites are easier to control with a bracket bonded to the tooth. Braces also don't depend on cooperation: nothing gets left out after lunch.

Healthy teeth, bone, and gums matter far more than age. If visibility is your only hesitation, ask about 3M Clarity Advanced ceramic brackets — same mechanics, tooth-colored material. Still deciding? Read Invisalign vs. braces, when braces are necessary, or five things adults should know.

  • Moderate to severe crowding — Overlapping teeth, or cases needing space created first.
  • Bite correction — Overbite, underbite, crossbite, or open bite, usually with elastics.
  • Rotated or tipped teeth — Movements needing a bracket's grip, not a tray's squeeze.
  • Kids and teens — Timing movement with jaw growth, often after Phase 1.
  • Adults with healthy gums and bone — There's no upper age limit.

Do braces hurt?

Putting them on doesn't hurt — no drilling, no numbing. Teeth feel tender for two or three days afterward and briefly after big wire changes; soft food and a pain reliever handle it.

How long will I wear braces?

Most comprehensive cases run roughly 18 to 30 months; simpler ones finish sooner. The more movement and bite correction needed, the longer it takes. You'll get a case-specific estimate at your consult.

How often are adjustment appointments?

Usually every six to eight weeks, about 15 to 20 minutes each. We change the wire and ties and check that teeth are tracking to plan. Missed visits are the main reason treatment runs long.

Am I too old for metal braces?

No. Teeth move at any age as long as gums and supporting bone are healthy, and adults are a large share of our braces patients. If appearance worries you, ask about ceramic brackets.

What can't I eat with braces?

Skip hard, sticky, and chewy foods — ice, nuts, hard candy, caramel, taffy, popcorn — which pop brackets off and bend wires. Cut apples and raw carrots into pieces instead.

What are self-ligating braces?

They hold the archwire with a built-in sliding gate instead of an elastic tie, so there's less to trap plaque and visits are often shorter. They still need periodic adjustments.

Will I need a retainer afterward?

Yes, and it isn't optional. Teeth drift for life, and a retainer is what holds the result you paid for. We scan for retainers the day your braces come off.
