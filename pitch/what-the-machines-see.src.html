Pew also found that searching for information is the single most common thing people use them for. That is the same job the search box used to do — which means an assistant is now standing between a patient and the list of orthodontists they choose from.

On the numbers: Pew Research Center surveyed 5,119 U.S. adults, 17–23 February 2026. This is national data — no Round Rock–specific survey of AI use exists, so I am not going to invent one. Round Rock skews higher in both household income and college education than the U.S. average, and Pew finds chatbot use rises with both, so local use is likely at least this high. That last step is an inference, not a measurement.

The new measure

Google now grades whether an AI agent can actually use your site.

PageSpeed Insights is the tool the whole industry uses to grade a website. For years it reported four scores: Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO. It now reports a fifth, sitting right beside them — Agentic Browsing.

It runs three checks per device, testing whether an automated agent can load your pages, understand what your practice is and what it offers, and reach the things a patient would want. Two devices, three checks each: six checks total.

Reading it fairly: this category is new and Google still labels it experimental, so it is not yet a ranking factor anyone can point to. It is a direct readout of machine-readability, which is exactly the thing that decides whether an assistant can describe your practice to a patient.

Checked live · 4 August 2026

Their admin username is published on the open internet.

  • ×The username is public. That endpoint hands out the administrator account name to anyone who visits it.
  • ×The login page is wide open. /blog/wp-login.php answers to the public internet, so there is a door to type that username into.
  • ×None of the four standard browser-security headers are set — no HSTS, no content-security policy, no clickjacking protection, no referrer policy.

Being precise, because this matters: no password is exposed and there is no sign anyone has broken in. What is exposed is the username — one of the two things needed to get in — next to an unlocked door. On the rebuild there is no login page and no admin account to find, because the site is published as plain files rather than run on WordPress.
