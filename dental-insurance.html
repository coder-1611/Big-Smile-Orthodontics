02 — How orthodontic benefits work

The four numbers that matter.

Lifetime max · Percentage · Waiting period · Age limit

Orthodontic coverage behaves differently from the rest of your dental plan. Four details decide what you actually pay, and we check all four for you:

  • Lifetime maximum — unlike annual dental limits, orthodontic benefits are usually capped once per person, forever. Once spent, they do not reset.
  • Coverage percentage — many plans pay a share of comprehensive treatment rather than a flat amount, up to that lifetime cap.
  • Waiting period — some plans require you to be enrolled for a set time before orthodontic benefits become active.
  • Age limits — certain plans cover dependents only, which matters if you are an adult considering Invisalign.

Orthodontic treatment is also generally an eligible FSA and HSA expense. Because FSA funds often expire at the end of the plan year, starting treatment before December can stretch your money further — worth asking about if you are on the fence.

What if Big Smile Orthodontics is out-of-network with my plan?

We still submit claims on your behalf and apply whatever your plan pays toward your balance. Most patients pay a similar amount either way once our in-house financing and new-patient offer are applied.

How much does dental insurance usually cover for orthodontics?

Orthodontic coverage is normally a separate lifetime benefit rather than an annual one, and many plans cover a percentage of comprehensive treatment up to a lifetime maximum. We will tell you the exact figure for your plan in writing at your consultation.

What is a lifetime orthodontic maximum?

It is the total amount your plan will ever pay toward orthodontic treatment for that person, not a yearly limit. Once it is used, it does not reset, which is why it is worth knowing the number before you start.

Do you offer payment plans if I have no insurance?

Yes. We offer in-house financing with $0 down and no interest, with monthly payments starting from $129, and we currently take $1,000 off comprehensive braces or Invisalign treatment.

Can I use an FSA or HSA to pay for braces?

In most cases yes, since orthodontic treatment is generally an eligible medical expense. Check with your plan administrator, and note that FSA funds often expire at year end, which can make a December start worthwhile.

Does insurance cover Invisalign the same as metal braces?

Most plans that cover comprehensive orthodontics treat Invisalign and braces the same way, though some older plans limit clear aligners. We verify this specifically before you commit.

Will you tell me the price before treatment starts?

Always. You leave your free consultation with a written treatment plan, the total fee, what your insurance is expected to pay, and your monthly payment. No surprises later.
