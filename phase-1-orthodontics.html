01 — What Phase 1 is

Early treatment, while the jaw is still growing.

Ages 7–10 · Mixed dentition · Growth guidance

Phase 1 — early, interceptive orthodontics — is for children with mixed dentition: baby and permanent teeth together. Treating at this stage lets Dr. Asrar guide how a smile develops instead of correcting it years later.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first check-up by age 7. Most seven-year-olds don't need braces — but by then enough permanent teeth have arrived to show how the jaws are growing. Timing matters: young jaws are still soft, so widening a narrow upper jaw is simple at eight and may take surgery at eighteen. That visit is free at our Round Rock office.

02 — Signs to watch for

What parents notice first.

Crowding · Crossbite · Habits · Airway

You don't have to diagnose anything — that's our job. Any one of these is reason enough for a visit.

  • Baby teeth lost early or late — both throw off a permanent tooth's path.
  • Difficulty chewing or biting — or foods quietly avoided.
  • Mouth breathing — plus chronic snoring or waking up tired.
  • Thumb or finger sucking past age four — steady pressure reshapes the bite.
  • Crowded or blocked-out teeth — a sign there isn't enough room.
  • Jaws that shift, click, or pop — or slide sideways on opening.
  • Teeth that meet abnormally, or not at all — front teeth never touching.
  • Crossbite — upper teeth biting inside the lowers.
  • Protruding front teeth — easier to chip or knock loose.

03 — Appliances & visits

What Phase 1 actually involves.

Palatal expander · Partial braces · Space maintainers

Phase 1 is short and targeted: one problem, one appliance, a clear finish line — rarely a full set of braces. Every plan starts with an exam, X-rays, and a digital iTero scan at our Round Rock office, then the cost in writing before anything begins.

  • Palatal expander — widens a narrow upper jaw a fraction of a millimeter at a time, fixing crossbite and making room for crowded teeth.
  • Partial braces — brackets on a few key teeth, to bring protruding front teeth back or free a stuck tooth.
  • Space maintainers — hold a gap open when a baby tooth is lost too soon, so the permanent tooth keeps its path.
  • Habit appliances — gentle reminders that help a child stop thumb sucking.

04 — Resting period & Phase 2

After Phase 1: resting, then Phase 2.

Resting period · Monitoring · Phase 2

When active treatment ends, most children get about a year appliance-free. It isn't idle time — the last baby teeth give way to permanent ones while we check eruption periodically, sometimes with a retainer holding the space we made.

Phase 2 starts once most permanent teeth are in, usually ages 11 to 13. It's the alignment stage — braces or Invisalign — and because the structural work is done, it's normally shorter.

We say this plainly: Phase 1 does not always eliminate the need for braces later. Its job is to fix what gets harder with age, so the second round is milder — and extractions or jaw surgery often come off the table entirely.

When should my child first see an orthodontist?

By age 7, per the American Association of Orthodontists. Most children that age need only a follow-up visit; the point is catching the few problems that are easier to fix while the jaws grow.

Will Phase 1 mean my child avoids braces later?

Sometimes, but we never promise it. Phase 1 reduces severity and can prevent extractions or surgery, though many kids still have a shorter, simpler round of braces or Invisalign as teens.

Does Phase 1 hurt?

Placing an expander or partial braces doesn't hurt. Expect pressure and tenderness for a few days as teeth start moving; soft foods handle it, and children adapt far faster than adults.

How long does Phase 1 take?

The active part usually runs several months to about a year, depending on what we're correcting. Then the appliance comes out and your child rests, with periodic checks, until Phase 2.

Is a palatal expander hard to get used to?

The first week is the adjustment — speech sounds different and eating takes care. Almost every child is back to normal within a week or two, and we show you how to clean it.

What does Phase 1 cost, and does insurance help?

It depends on the appliance and how long it's needed, so you get an exact figure at the free consultation. We're in network with most major plans and offer $0 down with no-interest monthly payments.
